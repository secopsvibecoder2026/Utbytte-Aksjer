About the company

Norsk Hydro was founded in 1905 by Sam Eyde and Kristian Birkeland, and the fertiliser business was spun off as Yara in 2004. What remains is an aluminium group that owns the chain from mine to finished profile. The bauxite is mined at Paragominas in the Brazilian state of Pará and refined into alumina at Alunorte, which Hydro itself describes as the world's largest alumina refinery outside China. Both were bought from the mining company Vale in 2011, together with the majority in the Albras smelter next door. Alumina is turned into metal at the plants in Norway and at Qatalum in Qatar, which Hydro half owns, among other places. At the far end of the chain Hydro makes profiles for buildings, cars and industry and remelts used aluminium.

Power and gas are the heavy costs. The Norwegian plants run on hydropower, partly from Hydro's own power stations and partly on long-term contracts. In 2025 Hydro secured power for Sunndal and Høyanger for the years 2031 to 2040 through two agreements with Hafslund, and the company is investing in the Illvatn pumped storage plant. Outside Norway, gas is the weak point. When gas deliveries in Qatar were cut in March 2026, Qatalum had to cut production sharply, and from August the same year Alunorte did not receive the gas its contract promised and had to buy at spot prices.

Under the dividend policy at least half of adjusted net income is to go to the ordinary dividend, measured over a full cycle, and a fixed minimum amount per share is the floor. In strong years buybacks or extra dividends come on top. Earnings follow the aluminium price on the London Metal Exchange, the alumina price and the krone exchange rate, so the dividend swings a great deal from year to year, while the floor limits how far it can fall. The state is the largest owner.

Tax for investors outside Norway

Norsk Hydro is a Norwegian company, and Norwegian companies deduct 25% withholding tax on dividends to shareholders resident abroad. If a tax treaty between Norway and your country gives a lower rate, you can get it when the dividend is paid by giving your custodian or account operator the documentation, or apply to the Norwegian Tax Administration for a refund afterwards, within five years. Shareholders resident in the EEA may instead use the risk-free return allowance where that gives less tax, and companies resident in the EEA may be exempt. Your own country may also tax the dividend.
