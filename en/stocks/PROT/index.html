About the company

Protector Forsikring does not sell insurance directly to the end customer. The company began writing non-life insurance in 2004, was listed in 2007 and sells only through insurance brokers and agents in every market. From Norway it entered Sweden in 2011, Denmark in 2012, Finland and the United Kingdom in 2016 and France in 2025. With no sales force of its own, its ambition is to have the lowest costs and the best quality in the market, and the company measures how satisfied the brokers are in a survey of its own.

The customers are businesses, the public sector and affinity schemes, meaning agreements set up for a particular group, such as the members of an organisation. Municipalities are a distinctive feature. In its annual report for 2025 Protector states that it is the largest insurer of municipalities and housing companies in Scandinavia, and the public sector and social housing are also a large part of the business in the United Kingdom. In 2025 France was still too small to give economies of scale, and the insurance operations there made a loss in their first year.

The second source of income is investments. Since 2014 Protector has managed its portfolio itself, with equities alongside bonds, and the company describes investing as a core business for an insurer. Profits therefore swing with the stock market as well as with claims. The board considers a distribution every quarter. The company must keep its solvency margin under Solvency II above a set minimum, and when capital is well above the target, the intention is to return the surplus as an extra dividend or share buybacks. A year with large claims or weak investment returns therefore quickly affects what can be paid out.

Tax for investors outside Norway

Protector Forsikring is a Norwegian company, and Norwegian companies deduct 25% withholding tax on dividends to shareholders resident abroad. If a tax treaty between Norway and your country gives a lower rate, you can get it when the dividend is paid by giving your custodian or account operator the documentation, or apply to the Norwegian Tax Administration for a refund afterwards, within five years. Shareholders resident in the EEA may instead use the risk-free return allowance where that gives less tax, and companies resident in the EEA may be exempt. Your own country may also tax the dividend.
