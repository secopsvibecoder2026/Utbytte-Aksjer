About the company

SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge is the result of two rounds of mergers three years apart. In 2021 SpareBank 1 BV in Sandefjord and Sparebanken Telemark merged to form SpareBank 1 Sørøst-Norge. On 1 October 2024 that bank was merged with SpareBank 1 SR-Bank, which changed its name to SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge and took the ticker SB1NO. The head office is in Stavanger, and the bank has offices in Rogaland, Vestland, Agder, Telemark, Vestfold, Buskerud, Akershus and Oslo.

The largest part of the bank is SR-Bank, which was established in 1976 as Sparebanken Rogaland when 22 local savings banks in the county merged. The oldest of them was founded in Egersund in 1839. Sparebanken Rogaland was one of the founders of the SpareBank 1 alliance in 1996, and in 2012 the bank converted from an equity certificate bank into a public limited company. That is why SB1NO trades as ordinary shares and not as equity certificates, the ownership instruments savings banks issue instead of ordinary shares. Savings bank foundations are nonetheless the largest owners, and the dividends they receive are passed on as donations to clubs, associations and voluntary work in the region.

Besides the bank, the group owns EiendomsMegler 1 Sør-Norge and SR-Boligkreditt, which buys mortgages from the bank and funds them with covered bonds. In 2026 the bank agreed to sell the majority of the accounting firm SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge Forretningspartner to ECIT. The bank is also a major owner in SpareBank 1 Gruppen, which holds stakes in Fremtind Forsikring and Kredinor, and in BN Bank. The dividend policy is to pay out around half of annual profit in cash, with buybacks of its own shares as a supplement. How much is paid out is weighed against capital requirements and the bank's own growth plans.

Tax for investors outside Norway

SpareBank 1 Sør-Norge is a Norwegian company, and Norwegian companies deduct 25% withholding tax on dividends to shareholders resident abroad. If a tax treaty between Norway and your country gives a lower rate, you can get it when the dividend is paid by giving your custodian or account operator the documentation, or apply to the Norwegian Tax Administration for a refund afterwards, within five years. Shareholders resident in the EEA may instead use the risk-free return allowance where that gives less tax, and companies resident in the EEA may be exempt. Your own country may also tax the dividend.
