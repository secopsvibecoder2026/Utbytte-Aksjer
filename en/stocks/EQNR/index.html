About the company

Equinor was founded by the Norwegian state as Statoil in 1972. In 1981 it became the first Norwegian company to be made operator of a field, Gullfaks. The company was listed in 2001, merged with Norsk Hydro's oil and gas business in 2007 and took the name Equinor in 2018. The state is the largest owner through the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries, and a parliamentary resolution from 2001 says the state shall own at least two thirds of the company.

The Norwegian continental shelf is the backbone of the business, and under the strategy presented at the 2026 capital markets day it is to receive most of the investment up to 2030. From there Equinor sells oil, pipeline gas and LNG, mostly to Europe. Outside Norway it produces oil and gas in the United States, Brazil, Angola, the United Kingdom and Canada, among other countries. On top of that comes a power business with offshore wind and other generation that is still small next to oil and gas, and a trading arm that buys and sells oil, gas and power.

The cash dividend is declared quarterly in US dollars. The krone amount is converted at Norges Bank's dollar rate around the record date, so a Norwegian shareholder can receive a different amount in kroner from one quarter to the next even when the dollar dividend is unchanged. The aim is to raise the quarterly dividend per share every year. The rest of what the company wants to return goes to share buybacks, and their size follows oil and gas prices and the strength of the balance sheet. The state takes part in the buybacks through the redemption of a proportional share of its own shares, so that its stake stays the same. Shares that are bought back are cancelled, which means later dividends are shared among fewer shares.

Tax for investors outside Norway

Equinor is a Norwegian company, and Norwegian companies deduct 25% withholding tax on dividends to shareholders resident abroad. If a tax treaty between Norway and your country gives a lower rate, you can get it when the dividend is paid by giving your custodian or account operator the documentation, or apply to the Norwegian Tax Administration for a refund afterwards, within five years. Shareholders resident in the EEA may instead use the risk-free return allowance where that gives less tax, and companies resident in the EEA may be exempt. Your own country may also tax the dividend.
