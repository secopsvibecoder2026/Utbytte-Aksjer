About the company

BW LPG owns and operates very large gas carriers, known as VLGCs, which carry liquefied propane and butane over long ocean distances. The company describes itself as the world's largest owner and operator of such ships. Its history goes back to the Bergesen shipping company in Stavanger, which entered gas shipping in 1978. The Sohmen family bought a majority of Bergesen in 2003, and the business became BW. The LPG business was spun off and listed on Oslo Børs in 2013. In 2024 the share was also listed in New York, the company moved its legal domicile from Bermuda to Singapore, and it bought twelve ships from Avance Gas. BW Group is still the largest shareholder.

A large part of the fleet can run on LPG as fuel. Alongside the shipping business, BW LPG has a trading arm, Product Services, which buys and sells LPG cargoes and hedges prices with derivatives. It earns its money on price differences between markets, and its reported profit can swing sharply from quarter to quarter because open positions are valued at current prices. The company also owns a majority of BW LPG India, which carries gas in Indian waters.

The dividend is paid every quarter and is calculated from the profit of the shipping business, not the trading arm. The guideline is half of that profit, raised to three quarters or all of it when net leverage is low enough. The trading arm counts only through what it passes on to the parent company. The board may also adjust for extraordinary items, such as gains when older ships are sold. The dividend is set in dollars and converted into kroner for shareholders on Oslo Børs, so the krone amount also moves with the exchange rate.

Tax for investors outside Norway

BW LPG is registered in Singapore. Norwegian withholding tax is deducted by Norwegian companies, so the rules of Singapore apply instead. Singapore does not withhold tax on dividends. Your own country may also tax the dividend.
