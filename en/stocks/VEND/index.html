About the company

Vend Marketplaces is the listed company that remained when Schibsted split up. The media houses were spun off in 2024, the company changed its name from Schibsted Marketplaces to Vend in 2025, and that autumn the A and B shares were merged into a single share class with one vote per share. Since then the share has traded under the ticker VEND. The foundation Stiftelsen Tinius is still the largest shareholder.

What remains is digital marketplaces in the Nordic countries, divided into mobility, real estate, jobs and recommerce. The well-known brands are FINN in Norway, Blocket in Sweden, DBA and Bilbasen in Denmark, and Tori and Oikotie in Finland. In real estate FINN is the clear market leader in Norway. Most revenue comes from listings paid for by car dealers, estate agents and employers, and it follows activity in the housing, car and labour markets. A growing share comes from transactions, as when a private individual sells a car to a dealer through Nettbil or lets a home through Qasa. Alongside its operations, Vend owns a minority stake in the marketplace group Adevinta.

The dividend policy is an ordinary dividend that is to rise gradually over time and is decided once a year. Free cash flow after dividends and investments is also returned to shareholders as share buybacks or extraordinary dividends, and buybacks have made up a large part of this. When Adevinta sold its Spanish business in 2026, Vend said its share of the proceeds paid out would be passed on to shareholders, mainly as buybacks. The dividend alone therefore shows only part of what the company returns.

Tax for investors outside Norway

Vend Marketplaces is a Norwegian company, and Norwegian companies deduct 25% withholding tax on dividends to shareholders resident abroad. If a tax treaty between Norway and your country gives a lower rate, you can get it when the dividend is paid by giving your custodian or account operator the documentation, or apply to the Norwegian Tax Administration for a refund afterwards, within five years. Shareholders resident in the EEA may instead use the risk-free return allowance where that gives less tax, and companies resident in the EEA may be exempt. Your own country may also tax the dividend.
